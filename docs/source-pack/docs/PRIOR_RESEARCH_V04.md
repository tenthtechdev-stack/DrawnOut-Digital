# DrawnOut Digital | Research, Creative Strategy and Pre-development Brief
**Version:** 0.4 | **Date:** 20 October 2026 | **Status:** Strategic direction recommended; client approval pending

## 1. Decision summary

**Recommended positioning:** An independent, creative-led, full-service digital agency: integrated brand identity, digital design, marketing and campaigns, words and storytelling, and creative direction. 'Full-service digital agency' is useful commercial positioning; 'independent creative and digital studio' is the more distinctive public-facing description proposed in the supplied copy. Both can coexist if delivery capabilities are verified.

**Market:** English-language, market-neutral presentation for clients in Pakistan and internationally. Do not invent international office locations, client origins or global credentials.

**Primary website objective:** Generate qualified project conversations by demonstrating capabilities, displaying original work, explaining how DrawnOut thinks and builds, and reducing friction at enquiry.

**Brand anchor (provided):** 'We make ideas impossible to ignore.' Use this as the preferred hero headline pending approval. Footer sign-off: 'Think it through. Draw it out. Make it matter.' The four-word line 'Design. Strategy. Storytelling. Digital.' may act as a compact capabilities descriptor.

**Proposed design direction:** Expressive editorial, portfolio-first, confident typography, modular composition, purposeful motion and clear navigation. A restrained site-level palette should allow varied client artwork to dominate. Do not lock colours or type until brand assets are approved.

**Initial implementation:** Codex-assisted development using Astro, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS and Git-managed structured project content; preview deployment and approval workflow; CMS/admin deferred. This is a recommendation, not an irrevocable stack selection.

## 3. Positioning and differentiators

The draft is strongest when it combines a clear conviction ('Design with intention', 'Good design gets attention. Great thinking gives people a reason to stay') with an accessible invitation ('You don't need a perfect brief'). Its differentiator should be **thinking made visible through creative execution**, not a promise that everything is available under one roof.

Recommended value proposition, for internal strategy: **DrawnOut shapes ideas into clear identities, persuasive stories and cohesive digital experiences.** Use the client's existing headline and service terminology publicly unless editorial revision is approved.

The draft asks the reader to believe five areas of expertise. Public service descriptions need verified deliverables and carefully selected relevant portfolio cases. Important specific gaps: (i) website development versus website mockup/design, (ii) advertising strategy and creative versus media buying/optimization, (iii) copywriting versus art directed layouts containing text, (iv) formal creative-direction role and ownership versus finished identity boards.

Great thinking gives people a reason to stay'; 'We don't start with templates. We start with questions' if factually accurate; 'You don't need a perfect brief'; 'Think it through. Draw it out. **Naming consistency issue:** Draft says 'DrawnOut', archive branding says 'DrawnOUT Digital' and the image contains the line 'From Dreams To Digital Success!' while the new copy proposes two newer brand lines. Confirm canonical brand name and logo, which tagline replaces which, and whether the portfolio identity is current or legacy. Do not silently choose the old brand artwork to control the website design.

## 6. Recommended website information architecture

**Primary navigation:** Home (via logo), Work, Services, About, Contact. Persistent CTA: 'Let's Talk' or 'Start a Project' (final wording with studio). This creates four explicit nav destinations without cluttering the header.

**Home:**
1. Hero headline 'We make ideas impossible to ignore' + descriptor and work/enquiry links.
2. Strong portfolio collage or featured-project transition, using real DrawnOut assets.
3. Compact services grid representing all five approved service headings.
4. Selected projects, ideally four to six, with clear project labels and concise role descriptions.
5. Short 'Not another creative agency' studio statement, emphasizing thinking.
6. Process teaser or evidence (only genuine, approved testimonials or outcomes).
7. Contact invitation 'Have a good idea? Let's make it better.'

**Work index:** Curated sortable projects; include meaningful filters only if there are sufficient entries. Avoid overcomplicated filters for six cases. Individual `/work/[slug]` pages use one reusable, content-driven case-study template.

**Services:** Five pillars as provided (Brand Identity & Design; Digital Design; Marketing & Campaigns; Words & Storytelling; Creative Direction). Detailed deliverables and proof links; avoid five thin SEO pages without unique content. Service pages can be introduced if validated scope and substantive content justify them.

**About:** 'Not Another Creative Agency', 'Pretty Isn't the Point', concise team and studio information, 'Small Details. Big Picture' process and 'Built for Brands Going Somewhere' audience-fit messaging. Real names and roles require confirmation.

**Contact:** 'Have a Good Idea? Let's Make It Better', welcoming plain-language enquiry instructions, email and form (name, email, project summary, optional service/timing/budget). Implement secure delivery, anti-spam, accessible validation, required privacy notices and a thank-you state.

**Footer:** Consistent brand naming, approved studio sign-off, social links, contact and necessary legal links.

## 7. Visual system recommendation

**Direction:** Expressive editorial with functional restraint, not a generic SaaS or agency template. Strong intentional typographic hierarchy, large hero statement, well-composed project imagery, flexible asymmetric grids, substantial negative space, elegant hover states and selectively bold section transitions.

**Site palette:** Start neutral until current brand assets are signed off; if orange is retained, treat it as the recognizable site accent, not an all-page background. Let Bunzo's red, SOL's green/yellow, Triarch's monochrome and other client identities retain their own colours without competing against the agency chrome.

**Typography:** Pair a distinctive, licensed headline face with a highly readable body face. Avoid committing to exact fonts from the portfolio boards; those are client-system fonts and not necessarily DrawnOut's brand fonts.

**Motion:** Reveal and transition effects should support attention and project comprehension. Avoid autoplay-heavy hero video, scroll hijacking, inaccessible cursor interactions and heavy animation across all sections. Respect reduced-motion settings.

**Responsive principle:** Mobile design is first-class: typography should remain clear and images containing tiny typesetting should be replaced by individual images/crops with accompanying HTML text.

## 8. Commercial, trust and SEO requirements

**Target clients:** Pakistan and international, primarily businesses launching brands, refreshing identities, creating digital properties or coordinating ongoing campaigns. Do not assert a portfolio of international clients unless verified. Real operating location can appear on Contact/About without limiting appeal.

**Conversion:** Two primary journeys, work-led ('Explore Our Work') and conversation-led ('Have Something in Mind?'). Keep inquiry copy human and low-pressure. CTA labels may vary by placement but destination and expectation should be consistent.

**Trust evidence:** Authentic approved project credits, client testimonials if available, named team and roles, explained process and actual contact details. Omit fabricated awards, unverified client outcomes, generic stock testimonials and mockup metrics.

**Organic search:** Brand-specific metadata; single-page focus for Home/Work/About/Contact; descriptive case-study titles and captions; image alt text; canonical URLs, sitemap and indexing rules; eligible Organization structured data with verified details; no location-spam landing pages or mass-generated thin content. Publish genuinely useful case studies before an unused blog.

**Success measures:** Qualified contact submissions, confirmed enquiries, portfolio project-page engagement, organic search impressions and nonbrand discovery. Define tracking and consent appropriately and set quantitative targets after baseline data.

## 9. Technical and publishing recommendation

Codex-assisted Astro + TypeScript + Tailwind CSS with Astro Content Collections to manage portfolio entries in Git. Store each project with structured schema fields: `slug`, `title`, `clientName`, `projectStatus` (client / internal / concept), `summary`, `year` (optional), `services[]`, `industry`, `coverImage`, `gallery[]`, `challenge`, `approach`, `deliverables[]`, `outcome` (optional), `credits[]`, `featured`, `order`, `published`, `approvalStatus`, `seoTitle`, `seoDescription`.

Never fill unprovided attributes with fictional values. Use strict validation and make `published` false by default. Require explicit approval for public project status, image usage and case-study text.

Flow: team provides original project files and brief -> Codex creates or updates content and optimized assets on a branch -> preview deployment -> reviewer checks content, credits, permissions and visuals -> merge -> publish. Astro content collections permit local structured content and future external CMS integration. CMS/admin is deferred; evaluate a Git-backed editor or full CMS only when nontechnical editors or higher publication frequency require it.

Performance/accessibility baseline: optimize images, serve appropriate formats/resolutions, avoid shipping entire board images as oversized thumbnails, strive for good Core Web Vitals, accessible forms/keyboard navigation, sufficient colour contrast, semantic headings and `prefers-reduced-motion` support. Confirm hosting and email/anti-spam integrations before build.
